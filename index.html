    ℹ️ About this app

    What it does

    It helps you spot plants that might be invasive (plants from other places that crowd out Wisconsin's own plants) and takes you to the Wisconsin DNR's page about them.

    How to use it

    1. Photo: take a clear, close photo of the leaves or flowers.
    2. Location: tap "Find me" or tap the map where the plant is.
    3. What plant is it? The Pl@ntNet AI guesses, and we check Wisconsin's invasive plant list.
    4. Learn more: we take you to the DNR's page about your plant, and show where you found it.

    The AI can be wrong

    That's why we always say "possible" and show how sure the AI is. Experts at the DNR make the final call. You can learn about any plant on the DNR's website, even if the app isn't sure.

    Stay safe

    Don't touch plants you don't know. Wild parsnip and giant hogweed can burn skin, and poison hemlock is very poisonous. Explore with an adult and stay on public land.

    Where your photo and location go

    • Your photo is made smaller and sent through our plant helper to Pl@ntNet to be identified. Nothing is saved, and there's no login.
    • Your location stays on your phone. We show it to you at the end, in case you report the plant to the DNR.
    • The map pictures come from OpenStreetMap, so they can tell roughly which area you're looking at.

    Reporting to the DNR

    This app never sends anything. To report a plant, use the DNR's own way: DNR: Report invasive species
