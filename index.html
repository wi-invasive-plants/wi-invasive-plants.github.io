    ℹ️ About this app
    

      
    What it does
    
      
    It helps you spot plants that might be invasive (plants from other places that crowd out
        Wisconsin's own plants) and report them to the Wisconsin DNR.
    

      
    How to use it
    
      
      
        
    1. Photo: take a clear, close photo of the leaves or flowers.
      2. 
        
    2. Location: tap "Find me" or tap the map where the plant is.
      3. 
        
    3. What plant is it? The Pl@ntNet AI guesses, and we check Wisconsin's invasive plant list.
      4. 
        
    4. Report: we write the email. You attach the photo, read it, and press Send.
      5. 
      
    

      
    The AI can be wrong
    
      
    That's why we always say "possible" and show how sure the AI is.
        Experts at the DNR make the final call. You can report any plant, even if the app isn't sure.
    

      
    Stay safe
    
      
    Don't touch plants you don't know. Wild parsnip and giant hogweed can burn skin, and poison hemlock is very
        poisonous. Explore with an adult and stay on public land.
    

      
    Where your photo and location go
    
      
      
        
    • Your photo is made smaller and sent through our plant helper to Pl@ntNet to be identified.
          Nothing is saved, and there's no login.
      • 
        
    • Your location stays on your phone until you send the email yourself.
      • 
        
    • The map pictures come from OpenStreetMap, so they can tell roughly which area you're looking at.
      • 
      
    

      
    Practice or real?
    
      
    When you see 🧪 Practice at the top, reports go to a pretend address (test@example.com),
        so they never bother the DNR. Our team decides when the app sends real reports. Samples are always practice.
    

      
    How reports reach the DNR
    
      
    The DNR asks people to email Invasive.Species@wisconsin.gov with photos.
        DNR: Report invasive species
